Why Phantoma

Dev blog · April 20, 2026

I built Phantoma because the games I keep coming back to aren't the ones with the best combat or the tightest progression loops. They're the ones with spaces that feel lived in. PlayStation Home, early WoW capital cities, Habbo, VRChat on a good night — places where the point was the people and the vibe, not the objective.

Most modern games have forgotten how to be hangout spaces. Everything is a battle pass, a ranked ladder, a grind. Phantoma is trying to be the opposite of that: a small, beautiful, character-driven world where the reward for showing up is just being there. The characters are expressive, the world has personality, and the design bias is always toward "what would make this feel like a place I want to exist in" instead of "what would make this more engaging per session."

What makes it unique is the character pipeline. Every character in Phantoma has real animation weight behind it — idles with personality, walk cycles that feel like the character is someone, emotes that aren't canned Fortnite moves. That's the thing I care about most, and it's the thing that'll make Phantoma feel different the second you drop in.

The Unity refactor I'm doing for this class deliverable is actually a real branch of the project, not throwaway prototype code. Which means this assignment is pulling double duty — it counts for school, and it moves the actual game forward.
