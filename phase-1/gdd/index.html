Phantoma · Draft · April 20, 2026

1. Title

Phantoma

2. Genre

Social hub / multiplayer exploration. Closest neighbors are social sandbox games and lobby-based MMOs, but the vibe leans more toward "explorable character showcase."

3. Platform

Primary: PC (Windows) via Steam and itch.io — that's where the audience for small character-driven multiplayer games lives, and the tech stack is easiest to iterate on. Secondary: Mac, since the dev environment already supports it and the overhead to ship there is low.

4. Premise

A hub world where the characters have soul and the space has memory. Show up, explore, express, belong.

5. Backstory

Phantoma is a world between places — a quiet hub that characters drift into when they're between adventures. Nobody knows exactly how they got there, and nobody's in a hurry to leave. The world has been shaped by the characters who've passed through it, so every corner has traces of someone who was there before you.

6. Target Rating

ESRB Teen (T). Mild stylized themes, online interaction with other players (the main driver of the rating), no combat violence, no strong language in first-party content. The online interaction factor is what pushes it out of E10+.

7. Target Market

Players aged 16–30 who gravitate toward social games, character customization, and comfy multiplayer experiences. Overlap with audiences for VRChat, Animal Crossing, Final Fantasy XIV social play, and PlayStation Home nostalgics. Skews toward players who value presence and expression over competition.

8. Player Motivation

Driven by exploration, expression, and social discovery. There is no win state — the draw is finding hidden areas, unlocking new ways to present your character, and running into other players in a space that feels worth being in. Motivation comes from curiosity and belonging, not scoreboards.

9. Unique Selling Proposition

Phantoma's edge is character soul. Most social hub games treat characters as dress-up dolls with canned animations. Phantoma treats every character as a performer — idles, walks, and emotes are designed and animated with real intent, so your character feels like someone the moment you spawn in. Combined with a hub world designed for hanging out rather than grinding, that's the hook: a place where being there is the point, and the characters make being there feel good.

10. Competitive Analysis

VRChat. The closest spiritual competitor — a social-first space where expression and presence matter more than objectives. VRChat's strength is openness; its weakness is that quality is wildly inconsistent because everything is user-generated, and onboarding is rough. Phantoma competes by offering a curated, polished experience with the same social-first DNA but a consistent aesthetic and animation quality floor. Players who want the VRChat vibe without the chaos are the sweet spot.

Animal Crossing: New Horizons. A comfy, low-stakes, expression-driven experience with a devoted audience. Its weakness is that multiplayer is clunky and async — friends can visit, but there's no real "hanging out" layer. Phantoma competes by being real-time multiplayer-native while keeping the comfy, low-pressure tone Animal Crossing audiences love.

Final Fantasy XIV (social play). A huge chunk of FFXIV's player base spends time in hub cities roleplaying, socializing, and showing off glamours rather than raiding. That's a tell about what players actually want from a shared space. FFXIV's weakness is that all of that lives on top of a massive MMO subscription and grind. Phantoma competes by offering the social-hub experience as the main course, stripped of the grind and the paywall.

11. Goals

The experience I'm chasing is belonging, curiosity, and quiet joy. Not excitement, not tension — closer to walking into a familiar bar where the bartender nods at you, or finding a quiet corner of a park on a good afternoon. I want players to log in and feel like the world is glad they're back.

Specific mood targets: warm, a little wistful, curious, inviting. The game achieves this through deliberate animation personality on every character, lighting and audio that prioritize atmosphere over spectacle, and a design philosophy that never pressures the player toward an objective. The player's own stories — who they met, what they found, what their character became — are the actual content.
